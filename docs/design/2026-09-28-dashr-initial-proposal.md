## 1. Answer

1. **Render speed.** DASHR is not a speed technique for Matter. Each DASHR march
   iteration does six filtered reads, four of them 128-bit
   (section 4). A Matter chart-march step costs about the same. The measured POM
   cost comes from how Matter's own march is built:
   - a crease rule that marks bands around almost every bent triangle edge. On
     curved surfaces that can send most pixels to the expensive triangle walk,
     which was meant as a rare fallback. Phase 0 measures the share;
   - a 30-step minimum on every POM pixel;
   - a full virtual-texture resolve and 4-texel validation on every step;
   - no empty-space skipping;
   - the same march on every secondary ray-tracing hit.

   Fixing these (Phase A) is the render-speed work. It needs no DASHR code.
   Section 2.1 sets the ceiling: with POM switched off at runtime, the settled
   StreamMountain G-buffer still costs 302 ms. That figure still pays for the
   compiled-in POM code's registers (S16), which has not been measured.
2. **Robustness.** First, stop tagging gentle bends as creases (A0). Then adopt
   DASHR's *seam teleport*: a signed distance to the chart edge, plus a
   destination across the seam or sharp crease. Store it as VT page data
   generated by the existing page compositor. Re-project the 3D ray point at
   each crossing, rather than carrying height as DASHR does. This replaces the
   connected triangle walk (Phase B), whose cost is unbounded in practice and
   which needs rest-pose triangles at runtime.
3. **Animated meshes.** Skinned draws have no VT slot today, so they get no VT
   shading and no POM at all. For material-scale relief, animated POM needs
   plumbing, not DASHR's solver:
   - a VT slot on the skinned draw;
   - a skinned tangent frame;
   - posed edge endpoints and normals from the skin output, read only when a
     ray crosses a seam (Phase C).

   DASHR re-renders a full UV-space mapping atlas every frame. That cannot scale
   to Matter's sparse 16384² virtual atlases, and it is not needed at this relief
   scale.
4. **VT/VG.** POM v2 lives inside VT pages, which the same background compute
   produces. Phase B also removes the dependency that currently forces POM off
   whenever VT pages come from the disk cache. VG is not a dependency: POM v2
   needs only atlas UVs and normals on whatever geometry is drawn. It neither
   rescues VG nor waits for it.
5. **Scope.** Ground (Wang tileset) POM and the surface-detail marcher are
   separate code paths (section 3). Phases A–C target VT chart POM. The
   ray-tracing policy (A6) also covers the surface-detail path. The gate (A5)
   reaches ground POM only after the ray-tracing origin lifts are gated per
   pixel.

## 2. Adversarial render-speed review

Every claim below is tested against one question: does this lower whole-frame GPU
time at matched image quality?

### 2.1 What the measurements allow

Source: attribution report §2 (RTX 4090, 1920×1080, RT/GI on, no DLSS).

| Window | Zone | POM off | POM reference | Delta |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 300 s warmup | total GPU (median) | 416.55 ms | 693.55 ms | +277.0 ms |
| 300 s warmup | `gbuffer` | 302.24 ms | 523.22 ms | +221.0 ms |
| 300 s warmup | `rt_gi` | 92.67 ms | 149.04 ms | +56.4 ms |
| 45 s warmup | `gbuffer` | 145.53 ms | — | +23.1 ms (reference), +16.4 ms (chart-only) |

What these numbers support and what they do not:

- **The POM delta grew about tenfold** between the 45 s and 300 s windows. The
  report reads this, without isolating it, as POM cost following resident fine
  terrain and filled VT pages.
- **The split between chart-local and connected POM is known only at 45 s.**
  There the connected walk was not separable from noise. At 300 s only the
  reference variant was captured, so its split is unknown (findings S4, S15).
  `chart_only` also differs from `reference` in its result, not only its cost:
  it shades crease-band pixels flat.
- **The delta does not say which marcher costs what.** One enable drives the
  ground tileset, VT chart and surface-detail marchers together (S17).
- **POM is the largest single toggle, not the whole frame.** Switching POM off at
  runtime still leaves a 416 ms frame with a 302 ms G-buffer. That run still
  compiles the POM code into the G-buffer shader, so it pays some register and
  occupancy cost of its own (S16). The attribution report ranks "split the
  POM-off G-buffer into geometry versus shading" second, after POM (§6 item 2).
  That work is outside this proposal, but it bounds any whole-frame claim made
  here.
- **Small samples.** The windows were 27–66 frames long and were not fully
  settled. Tail percentiles from them are weak evidence, and pass medians must
  not be summed.
- **Ordinary StreamMountain runs have POM off.** The world saves
  `render.pom.enabled=false`. The engine default is on
  ([world_definition.h:301](../../MatterEngine3/include/matter/world_definition.h)).

### 2.2 Findings

Severity follows the gate policy: Critical, High, Medium or Low. "Phase" names
where section 5 addresses each finding. Every citation is to the current Matter
source unless it says DASHR.

Findings are listed by severity, not by ID. S15–S17 came from the independent
adversarial pass (Appendix A). S1–S14 were revised after it.

**S15 — Critical — The crease rule can send most curved-surface pixels to the
connected walk.** (Phase 0, A0, B)
- **Evidence:** the compositor tags a texel 4 ("interior crease") when it lies
  within `relief·tan(θ/2) + 1.5·footprint` of any triangle edge whose dihedral
  cosine is below 0.99999, about a 0.26° bend
  (`vt_composite.comp:393-413`). The chart-local march accepts only tag 2, at
  the origin and at every bilinear footprint
  ([vt_parallax.glsl:47-51, 141](../../MatterEngine3/shaders_vk/vt_parallax.glsl)).
  Every tag-4 pixel therefore reports `INITIAL_BOUNDARY` or `PATH_BOUNDARY` and
  enters the connected walk.
- **Consequence:** the fixed 1.5-texel term applies at any bend angle. On a
  faceted terrain or a curved creature, where triangles are a few texels wide,
  most texels can carry tag 4. The connected walk may be the main path, not the
  fallback the design assumed. This is inference; Phase 0 measures the tag-4
  area and the boundary-status share.
- **Correction:**
  - Tag a crease only where the miter shift `relief·tan(θ/2)` exceeds about half
    a texel. Keep the footprint padding only around those edges.
  - Let the affine march cross gentle bends. This is the same local-affine
    approximation the chart march already makes.
  - Phase B must handle the remaining sharp creases inside a chart without
    splitting charts. Splitting would take a whole page per small chart
    (`mesh_charting.h:178-184`).

**S1 — High — The step count has a 30-step floor, borrowed from the ground
knob.** (Phase A1)
- **Evidence:** `vt_parallax_chart_sample` computes the step count as
  `clamp(max(steps, needed), 1, 128)`
  ([vt_parallax.glsl:145-154](../../MatterEngine3/shaders_vk/vt_parallax.glsl)).
  `needed` already includes the module-material gradient (`:151-152`) and is the
  half-texel step count at the requested mip. `steps` is `pom_a.x`, 30 by
  default ([gbuffer.frag:995-998](../../MatterEngine3/shaders_vk/gbuffer.frag),
  [world_definition.h:305](../../MatterEngine3/include/matter/world_definition.h)).
  That value is the ground tileset march's real step count; the VT march reuses
  it as a minimum. The connected loop also divides its march into at least
  `steps` pieces
  ([vt_parallax.glsl:253-273](../../MatterEngine3/shaders_vk/vt_parallax.glsl)).
  When `needed` exceeds 128, the code shortens the ray (`end_t`) rather than
  lengthening the step. Spacing therefore never exceeds half a texel.
- **Consequence:** the loop stops at the first crossing (`:159-175`), so the
  cost is about `count × (hit depth / relief)`. Even so, a march spanning two
  texels divides its ray into 30 pieces instead of about 4, and pays up to 30
  full depth evaluations.
- **Correction:** the VT march derives its count from `needed`, with a small
  floor. Keep the 128 cap and the ray shortening beyond it. Give the VT march
  its own cap, separate from the ground knob.

**S16 — High — POM's register cost lands on every G-buffer fragment, and a
delta gate cannot see it.** (Phase 0, section 7)
- **Evidence:** POM lives in the G-buffer uber-shader. Only the connected path
  can be removed by specialisation constant; the source says removing it "also
  exposes its register/code cost"
  ([gbuffer.frag:25-28](../../MatterEngine3/shaders_vk/gbuffer.frag)). Runtime
  POM-off only zeroes `pom_a.x`.
- **Consequence:**
  - The POM-off baseline still pays POM's static register allocation, so the
    measured delta understates POM's full cost.
  - A change that adds loop state (A2) could slow every fragment. The POM delta
    would shrink while the frame slows.
- **Correction:** gate on absolute POM-on time, plus no regression in POM-off
  time. Add a variant that compiles all POM out, and record register counts.

**S2 — High — Every step re-resolves and re-validates the VT address.** (Phase A2/A3)
- **Evidence:** each `vt_parallax_depth` call
  ([vt_parallax.glsl:91-110](../../MatterEngine3/shaders_vk/vt_parallax.glsl))
  runs the following. For an ordinary finite receiver, that is about 6 texture
  operations and 3–4 dependent buffer loads per step, with an
  indirection→metadata→texture dependency chain.
  - `vt_resolve`: a variant record, an indirection entry, page-input fields and
    1 AUX fetch ([vt_common.glsl:321-330](../../MatterEngine3/shaders_vk/vt_common.glsl));
  - 4 AUX fetches for the bilinear footprint check (`:47-51`);
  - the envelope, snapshot and mapping comparisons;
  - 1 filtered height fetch.
- **Periodic module materials cost more.** `vt_apply_receiver_material` adds a
  load through the mapping pointer, a second variant record and
  `vt_resolve_periodic`, which retries up to 9 mips. Height then comes from the
  module page, through an affine map with wrap-around
  ([vt_common.glsl:264-310](../../MatterEngine3/shaders_vk/vt_common.glsl)).
- **Consequence:** the performance review's P0-1 already found this. Three of the
  five operations are loop-invariant while the ray stays inside one page.
- **Correction:** resolve once per page crossing and march in physical page
  coordinates (work-queue Tier 1.3), tracking the module page separately when
  there is one. The attribution report ranks it first.

**S3 — High — There is no empty-space skipping.** (Phase A4)
- **Evidence:** the linear march has no min/max, cone or hierarchical height
  data. `height_output` (`vt_composite.comp:105`) is the source's *declared*
  range, not a measured per-page range, and it feeds only the height decode.
- **Consequence:** rays above flat or low regions pay every half-texel step.
- **Correction:** add conservative per-page and per-tile bounds, written by the
  compositor at page-fill time.

**S4 — High — The connected walk's cost is unbounded in practice and unmeasured
at steady state.** (Phase B)
- **Evidence:** on a chart boundary or crease, `vt_parallax_sample` switches to
  up to 128 steps of `vt_walk_locate` plus refinement
  ([vt_parallax.glsl:224-296](../../MatterEngine3/shaders_vk/vt_parallax.glsl)).
  - `vt_walk_locate` takes up to 32 hops of 112-byte triangle reads with 3
    neighbour tests each
    ([vt_surface_walk.glsl:188-252](../../MatterEngine3/shaders_vk/vt_surface_walk.glsl)).
  - A cross-owner neighbour adds a binary search and a scan of up to 64 links of
    176 bytes (`:65-107`).
  - A manual bilinear sample does 4 taps, each re-walked by
    `vt_walk_filter_point` (32 hops, which can re-enter `vt_walk_locate`)
    (`:278-348, 350-417`).
  - Per-pixel seeding walks a BVH, or scans up to 2048 triangles linearly
    ([vt_seed_walk.glsl:28-78](../../MatterEngine3/shaders_vk/vt_seed_walk.glsl)).
- **Unmeasured:** the 300 s share is unknown. Given S15, it may cover most
  curved-surface pixels.
- **Structural cost:** the walk needs rest-pose triangles leased at runtime. That
  blocks skinned meshes (S13) and the disk cache (S14), and makes POM on VG
  terrain pages march against source triangles instead of the drawn ones.

**S5 — High — Secondary ray-tracing hits run full POM.** (Phase A6)
- **Evidence:** GI, reflection and refraction rays reach `rt_surface.rchit`.
  `apply_rt_composed_height` calls `vt_parallax_sample` with the scene's step
  settings
  ([rt_surface_common.glsl:386-403](../../MatterEngine3/shaders_vk/rt_surface_common.glsl)).
  Ray tracing never defines `VT_POM_CONNECTED_ENABLED`, so the connected walk is
  always enabled there.
- **Consequence:** `rt_gi` rises by 56 ms at 300 s. A diffuse GI hit, sampled at
  one ray per pixel or fewer, gains little from resolving relief below 0.35 m.
- **Existing controls:** `MATTER_RT_SURFACE_DETAIL_MODE` is environment-only
  ([vk_scene_renderer.cpp:3986-3997](../../MatterEngine3/src/render/vk_scene_renderer.cpp)).
  - Mode 2 (zero steps) reaches the VT path (`rt_surface_common.glsl:398`).
  - Mode 1 is a cone-footprint weight, `rt_surface_relief_weight`
    (`:423-437`). It applies only to the surface-detail path, never to
    `apply_rt_composed_height`.

**S6 — Medium — Overdraw multiplies march cost, and the factor is unknown.** (Phase 0, A7)
- **Evidence:** the march runs in the G-buffer fragment shader. There is no
  depth prepass: the occlusion ID raster runs at reduced resolution and culls
  whole draws, not pixels
  ([vk_scene_renderer.cpp:44-53](../../MatterEngine3/src/render/vk_scene_renderer.cpp)).
  Conservative `depth_less` output preserves early-Z
  ([gbuffer.frag:141-151](../../MatterEngine3/shaders_vk/gbuffer.frag)). But a
  fragment later covered by a nearer surface has already paid for its march.
- **Correction:** measure per-pixel march invocations before choosing a remedy.
  A pipeline-statistics query cannot isolate POM, because POM is a per-pixel
  branch inside one shader over indirect draws. Use a diagnostic specialisation
  that counts march entries per pixel.

**S7 — Medium — The gate measures relief against footprint, not parallax in
pixels.** (Phase A5)
- **Evidence:** fade is `smoothstep(0.25, 2.0, relief / resolved_m)` within the
  1564 m distance band
  ([vt_parallax.glsl:133-135, 228-230](../../MatterEngine3/shaders_vk/vt_parallax.glsl);
  [world_definition.h:306](../../MatterEngine3/include/matter/world_definition.h)).
- **Consequence:** at near-normal views, distant pixels with less than one pixel
  of lateral parallax still march, and with S1 they pay 30 steps. At grazing
  views the lateral shift can exceed a pixel even when relief is under one
  footprint.
- **Correction:** gate on a displacement bound that includes the depth term, as
  `rt_surface_relief_weight` already does for ray tracing
  (`rt_surface_common.glsl:423-437`).

**S8 — Medium — POM cost and quality are coupled to VT fill health.** (All phases)
- **Evidence:** the march needs resident direct-source pages, and misses become
  neighbour requests. VT `vt` p99 is 158–525 ms in all 8 runs, and fill frames
  cost 100–525 ms (attribution §2.1).
- **Consequence:** any new page channel adds to those bursts. New POM data must
  be cheap to derive (from geometry, not the material tape), or must be
  measured against the fill budget.

**S17 — Medium — The POM delta is not attributed to a marcher.** (Phase 0)
- **Evidence:** one enable (`pom_a.x`) drives the ground tileset, VT chart and
  surface-detail marchers together
  (`vk_scene_renderer.cpp:6465-6467`; `gbuffer.frag:609, 997, 1207`).
- **Consequence:** Phase A changes only the VT marcher. If ground or
  surface-detail POM dominates the +221 ms, A's gate cannot be met, and the
  reason would be invisible.
- **Correction:** Phase 0 runs one variant per marcher.

**S9 — Medium — DASHR's per-step reads are not cheaper, and for static geometry
its mapping is redundant.** (Section 4)
- **Evidence:** each DASHR iteration does 6 filtered reads: the teleport SDF,
  four RGBA32F distortion reads and the height (DASHR
  `PipelineMain.hlsl:235-373`). Matter charts are planar projections within a
  45° normal cone (`chart_atlas.h:36`). Matter already derives the local metric
  from screen derivatives
  ([vt_parallax.glsl:17-32](../../MatterEngine3/shaders_vk/vt_parallax.glsl)) and
  stores per-chart bases.
- **Correction:** do not port a per-texel distortion atlas for static relief.

**S10 — Medium — DASHR's step growth is not conservative.** (Phase A4)
- **Evidence:** DASHR's next step is `StepSize·max(1, −Δh·StepScale)`, which
  lengthens with height above the field (DASHR `PipelineMain.hlsl:365-366`). It
  can tunnel through thin features that Matter's half-texel march cannot miss.
- **Correction:** adopt skipping only with a conservative bound.

**S11 — Low — DASHR's stretch threshold may be off by 2×.**
- **Evidence:** the central difference spans two texels but is divided by one
  (DASHR `PipelineEdgefill.hlsl:116-128`). If so, damping is always active at
  rest. This comes from reading the source; the demo was not run.
- **Correction:** do not copy DASHR's thresholds without re-deriving them.

**S12 — Low — The same address is resolved several times per pixel before the
march.**
- **Evidence:** `vt_resolve` for the same `(slot, uv, lod)` runs at
  [gbuffer.frag:190-191](../../MatterEngine3/shaders_vk/gbuffer.frag) and again at
  `:976`. The compiler is not guaranteed to combine the buffer loads.
- **Correction:** hoist one result. The cost is small beside the march.

**S13 — High (robustness) — Skinned meshes get no VT shading and no POM.** (Phase C)
- **Evidence:** the skin transform tail is filled from `GpuDrawTransform{}`
  without a `vt_slot`
  ([vk_scene_renderer.cpp:15450-15468](../../MatterEngine3/src/render/vk_scene_renderer.cpp);
  [vk_scene_renderer.h:2464-2474](../../MatterEngine3/src/render/vk_scene_renderer.h)),
  so `gbuffer.frag:976` skips VT. Animated water is the same. No tangent is
  skinned
  ([animation_skin.comp:71-89](../../MatterEngine3/shaders_vk/animation_skin.comp)).
  The page-normal frame is a fixed function of the normal
  ([vt_normal_frame.glsl](../../MatterEngine3/shaders_vk/vt_normal_frame.glsl)).
- **Consequence:** "performant on animated meshes" starts from nothing. Once VT
  is enabled on skinned meshes, detail normals would twist about the normal
  under joint rotation.

**S14 — Medium (VT) — POM and the disk-backed VT page cache are mutually
exclusive.** (Phase B)
- **Evidence:** `MATTER_VT_ENCODED_CACHE` sets `tileset_pom_settings_.enabled = false`
  ([vk_scene_renderer.cpp:6739-6765, 13776](../../MatterEngine3/src/render/vk_scene_renderer.cpp)).
  Cached POM pages require the draw's chart and triangle geometry lease
  ([vt_encoded_upload.h:24-28](../../MatterEngine3/src/render/vt_encoded_upload.h)).
- **Consequence:** today, "streamed from disk" and POM cannot both be on.

**Against revision 1 itself:** its first four phases (rock asset, static raster,
RT seeding, streaming) touch none of S1–S7, where the measured cost lives. Its
animation phase came last. Revision 2 reorders the work.

### 2.3 Where the time would come from

This is the expected direction, not a forecast; the gates are in section 7.

| Lever | Today (source count) | After Phase A (and B) |
|---|---|---|
| Pixels sent to the connected walk | Every tag-4 pixel; the crease band follows almost every bent edge (S15) | Only pixels near bends whose miter shift exceeds about half a texel (A0). B replaces the walk |
| Steps per chart-march pixel | `max(30, needed)` up to 128, plus 4 refinements; stops at the first crossing | `needed` with a small floor, still capped at 128 with ray shortening; fewer after skipping |
| Work per step (finite receiver) | ~6 texture operations + 3–4 dependent buffer loads | ~1–2 texture operations inside a page; one resolve per page crossing (module receivers track a second page) |
| Seam or crease crossing | Connected walk: up to 32-hop locates per step | Phase B: a constant number of reads per crossing |
| Secondary RT hits | Full march plus connected walk | Cone-footprint weighting: little or no march for broad GI cones |

Phase A should at least halve the POM-on minus POM-off G-buffer gap without
slowing the POM-off or compiled-out variants. If it does not, the cost is
somewhere this model does not predict: overdraw, VT residency, another
marcher (S17), or occupancy (S16). Section 7 then stops the work for
investigation.

## 3. Current POM in Matter (reference)

| Path | Where it runs | Work per step | Limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| VT chart-local march, `vt_parallax_chart_sample` | G-buffer ([gbuffer.frag:976-1010](../../MatterEngine3/shaders_vk/gbuffer.frag)) and secondary RT hits | S2 | `max(steps, needed)` ≤ 128 steps + ≤ 12 refine (default 4) |
| Connected walk, `vt_parallax_sample` → `vt_walk_*` | After a chart-local boundary status, which includes every tag-4 crease band (S15) | S4 | 128 steps; 32 hops per locate; seed BVH, or a linear scan of up to 2048 triangles |
| Ground POM, `tileset_pom_march` | Wang-tile ground, G-buffer only | 1 `textureGrad` + 4 hashes (`tileset_common.glsl:609-684`) | `max(8, mix(full, 8, d/max_distance))` steps + refine; a horizon term |
| Surface-detail march, `surface_detail_sample` | Surface-detail materials; RT honours detail modes 0–2 | ≤ 3 triplanar fetches | ≤ 128 steps + ≤ 12 refine |

| datum bias | 0.168 m |

**Depth.** `gl_FragDepth` is written unconditionally, but declared `depth_less`
under reversed Z. Marched hits only move the surface away from the camera. There
is no silhouette relief: a march that exits without a hit falls back to flat
shading, and nothing is discarded.

**Where VT chart POM never runs:**
- skinned and animated-water draws (`vt_slot = 0`);
- VG prop pages, which have no chart table (`part_store.cpp:1393`);
- the encoded disk-cache mode.

**Instrumentation and tests that already exist:**
- `MATTER_GBUFFER_POM_PATH=reference|chart_only|work` selects specialisation
  constants and work counters
  ([gbuffer.frag:27-36](../../MatterEngine3/shaders_vk/gbuffer.frag);
  `vk_scene_renderer.cpp:4189-4208`).
- Vulkan smoke modes: `vt-pom-work`, `vt-composed-parallax`, `vt-composed-seam`,
  `vt-sector-seam`, `surface-parallax`, `vt-surface-connections`, `vt-rt`,
  `vt-normal-frame`
  ([vulkan_smoke_tests.cpp](../../MatterEngine3/tests/vulkan_smoke_tests.cpp),
  [vt_pom_work_tests.h](../../MatterEngine3/tests/vt_pom_work_tests.h)).
- For ground POM, `run-tilesetslicer` exercises a CPU mirror of the march.

**Charts and pages.** Charts are planar projections, segmented with a 45° normal
cone. Page-aligned packing guarantees that no finest-mip page spans two charts
(`mesh_charting.h`, `pack_charts_paged`). A chart seam at the finest mip is
therefore always a page change. Coarser mips can mix charts within one page.

**AUX tags.** Each texel's tag (`aux.a`) is one of:
- 1: flat source (no POM);
- 2: interior;
- 3: dilation or gutter;
- 4: crease band;
- 255: legacy.

The compositor assigns tag 4 near any bent edge (`vt_composite.comp:393-413`),
and the chart-local march accepts only tag 2.

## 4. What DASHR contributes

DASHR (*Dynamically Animated Skinned Heightfield Rendering*) marches one ray
through two coupled spaces: posed object space, where the ray is straight, and
surface space `(u, v, h)`, where the height field lives. It refines the mapping
and the height intersection in the same iteration.

**The march** (`PipelineMain.hlsl:235-373`). Each iteration:
1. **Teleport test:** a bilinear read of the seam SDF. When outside the chart, a
   point-sampled destination UV replaces the current UV.
2. **Step and re-project:** four RGBA32F bilinear reads give an interpolated
   inverse frame and anchor. It computes `S·(p − anchor) + (uv, 0.5)`.
3. **Escape tests.**
4. **Height:** one read.
5. **Hit:** a single secant interpolation.

That is 6–7 reads per iteration. The step grows linearly with height above the
field. The only real limit is a 10,000-iteration emergency cap. There is no
binary refinement, no mip use, no min/max hierarchy, no LOD selector and no
depth output. Misses are discarded, so its silhouettes come from the extruded
shell.

**Animation, every frame:**
- **Deform pass:** skins position, normal, tangent and bitangent, inverts
  `[T|B|N]` per vertex, and rasterises the result into four RGBA32F UV-space
  targets.
- **Edgefill pass:** 25 reads per mapping texel in mode 1. It also computes
  stretch values.

The cost scales with mapping resolution (256² by default), independent of screen
size. The paper warns: "Do not treat the demo as any sort of benchmark." It
gives no timings.

**Memory at demo formats:** 152 bytes per mapping texel, 9.5 MiB at 256². Of
that, 5.5 MiB is retained per unique static asset. Matter's VT stores
9 bytes per texel across all five channels (`vt_types.h:50-58`).

**Key claim.** Conventional POM "breaks down when the size of the heightfield
becomes significant compared to the size of the model," and has trouble with
seams. DASHR's contribution is solving the curved mapping and the seams together
for *large* relief on deforming meshes. Per-pixel relief on deforming meshes is
older ([Policarpo et al. 2005](https://www.inf.ufrgs.br/~oliveira/pubs_files/Policarpo_Oliveira_Comba_RTRM_I3D_2005.pdf)).
At Matter's material-relief scale (0.352 m cap on terrain, centimetres on a
creature), the local-affine approximation holds across a march. Seams and frames
are the parts that fail.

**License.** MIT-0 or Unlicense, chosen by the user, stated in the paper's
"License and misc" section. There is no LICENSE file. No upstream code is copied
by this proposal.

## 5. Proposed design: POM v2

### Phase 0 — Baseline (2–3 days)

All at one commit with the MSVC build. At least 200 sampled frames per variant,
with at least 3 repeats. Variants:
- 300 s warmup with `tools/streammountain_attribution.sh`: `pom_off`,
  `pom_reference`, `pom_chart_only`, `pom_work`.
- `MATTER_RT_SURFACE_DETAIL_MODE` 0, 1 and 2.
- **One variant per marcher (S17):** ground tileset only, VT chart only,
  surface-detail only. This needs a small diagnostic switch.
- **A compiled-out variant (S16):** a specialisation constant that removes every
  POM path from `gbuffer.frag`, compared with runtime POM-off. Record register
  and VGPR counts, via Nsight or `VK_KHR_pipeline_executable_properties`.

Counters, from an extended `GBUFFER_POM_WORK` specialisation:
- a histogram of `needed` and of steps taken;
- page crossings;
- status codes;
- the share of POM pixels on tag 4 and entering the connected walk (S15);
- march entries per pixel, to measure overdraw (S6).

Quality reference: the current code with a 128-step minimum and 12 refines, at
fixed views.

### Phase A — Make the existing march cheap (2–3 weeks; A0, A1 and A6 are days)

- **A0 — Crease threshold (S15).**
  - The compositor tags 4 only where `relief·tan(θ/2)` exceeds about half a
    texel at that page's texel size. Keep the footprint padding only around
    those edges.
  - Gentle bends go to the chart march, under the same local-affine
    approximation it already makes.
  - Accept by image diff against the Phase 0 reference at bend-rich views
    (creature, faceted terrain), plus the tag-4 and boundary-share counters.
- **A1 — VT step count (S1).**
  - The VT march's count becomes `clamp(needed, n_min, 128)`, with `n_min` of
    2–4. Beyond 128, keep shortening the ray.
  - Give it its own cap, separate from the ground knob, which remains the real
    ground step count. The same applies to the connected loop's nominal step.
  - Add `vt-pom-work` assertions: step spacing never exceeds half a texel at the
    requested mip, and a sub-texel ray takes at most `n_min` steps.
- **A2 — Page-local march (S2).**
  - The "page" is the indirection cell at the *desired* mip, so results match
    today's per-step resolve even when a coarser mip is resident.
  - Resolve once. Compute the ray-parameter interval that stays inside that
    cell's payload.
  - Step in physical coordinates, and hoist the envelope, snapshot and mapping
    checks per cell. Re-resolve only on a crossing.
  - Receivers with periodic module materials track two intervals: the receiver
    cell for coverage (AUX), and the module cell, with its wrap and range, for
    height.
  - Page borders hold real neighbouring texels, so bilinear taps stay inside
    the resolved page.
  - Watch loop-state registers (S16).
- **A3 — One-read validity (S2).**
  - Define validity as: no invalid texel (wrong chart, dilation or crease)
    within one texel (Chebyshev) of the sample's nearest texel.
  - The compositor must compute this with a small distance-transform pass over
    the page after classification. Interior texels do not know their edge
    distance today (`vt_chart_resolve.glsl:118-147`). Budget that pass against
    S8.
  - Store a clamped signed texel distance. Phase B needs more bits than
    `aux.a` has, so plan a new R8 (or wider) channel: +1 byte per texel over
    today's 9.
  - One read then replaces four `texelFetch` calls.
- **A4 — Conservative skipping (S3).**
  - While filling a page, the compositor writes the page's maximum height (its
    minimum depth) and a 17×17 grid of per-tile maxima (8-texel tiles, R16,
    578 bytes per page), each with a one-texel apron.
  - Skip a tile while the ray's depth stays shallower than the tile bound
    across the tile interval. This is conservative for the march's own
    UV-linear model; S10's unbounded step growth is rejected.
  - For periodic module receivers, place the bounds on module pages in module
    space. Coverage-only receiver pages carry no height (`vt_composite.comp:444-455`).
  - Start with the single per-page bound.
- **A5 — Displacement gate (S7).**
  - Replace `smoothstep(0.25, 2.0, relief/resolved)` in the raster VT march with
    the ray-tracing formulation: along-ray displacement, depth term included,
    over footprint (`rt_surface_relief_weight`, `rt_surface_common.glsl:423-437`).
    Keep `fade` out of its own formula.
  - Do not change ground-POM gating yet. Ground GI and shadow origins are lifted
    whenever POM is globally on (`rt_lighting_impl.glsl:1113-1114`,
    `rt_shadow.rgen:274-275`), and the code warns that lifting unrecessed pixels
    leaks contact shadows (`vk_scene_renderer.cpp:17503-17507`). Gate those
    lifts per pixel first.
- **A6 — Ray-tracing policy (S5).**
  - Apply mode 1's `rt_surface_relief_weight` to `apply_rt_composed_height`
    too, and make mode 1 the default. Broad GI cones then march little or not
    at all, and sharp reflections keep full relief. The single closest-hit
    shader cannot tell ray classes apart, but the payload carries `cone_width`.
  - A skipped march moves a hit by up to `min(relief/inward, 1.59 m)` along the
    ray. GI origins return to the proxy through ORM.a
    (`rt_lighting_impl.glsl:1103-1107`), so this adds no self-intersection risk.
  - Validate with the `vt-rt` smoke mode and GI image diffs.
- **A7 — Overdraw, only if Phase 0 shows more than about 1.3 march entries per
  covered pixel.**
  - A naive depth prepass does not work. The G-buffer writes marched depth that
    is farther than raster depth (`depth_less`, reversed-Z `GREATER_OR_EQUAL`),
    so every marched fragment would fail the test against its own prepass value.
  - Options:
    - front-to-back ordering of POM draws;
    - a prepass followed by an EQUAL test with depth writes off, then a fix-up
      pass that rebuilds depth from the displacement stored in ORM.a;
    - a deferred per-pixel march from a visibility buffer.
  - Each option adds vertex or pass cost to a geometry-heavy frame. Choose by
    measurement.
- **A8 — Hoist the duplicate resolves** (S12).

### Phase B — Robust seams and creases: VT teleport replaces the connected walk (3–4 weeks)

- **Page data.**
  - The A3 channel, widened, stores an edge index in dilation and crease-band
    texels.
  - Each page carries an **edge table**, holding only the chart-boundary and
    remaining sharp-crease edges that touch it. Each record holds:
    - the edge's UV segment on this side;
    - its **3D endpoints and vertex normals** in rest space;
    - the destination: a stable key (part hash, rung, chart) and the destination
      edge's UV segment. A crease stays in the same chart, with a new plane;
    - for skinned parts, the edge's vertex indices.
  - The compositor derives all of this from triangle adjacency (the
    `GpuTriGeometry` neighbour words) while filling the page. No material tape
    is evaluated.
- **March.**
  1. When the A3 distance says the ray has left valid texels, read the edge
     record.
  2. Rebuild the current 3D ray point from the source side's edge frame, then
     re-project it onto the destination side, using the interpolated vertex
     normals across the edge. This keeps the recess continuous.
     - DASHR simply carries height through a teleport, which suits its smooth
       per-vertex frames.
     - In Matter, charts can be up to 45° from their own average plane. Carrying
       height would shift the 3D point by `h·|n_A − n_B|`, about 0.18 m at
       0.35 m depth and a 30° bend.
  3. Resolve the destination page. At the finest mip a chart seam is always a
     different page.
  4. Recompute `uv_ray` and `inward` from the destination basis (static) or the
     posed edge frame (C4).
  5. Continue with A2–A4.
- **Missing destination pages:**
  - request them through the existing POM feedback pair
    (`out_vt_feedback`, [gbuffer.frag:1012-1015](../../MatterEngine3/shaders_vk/gbuffer.frag));
  - fall back to flat for the rest of the ray, as missing neighbours do today;
  - cap crossings per ray (proposal: 4).
- **Cross-part seams** (terrain sectors, welds). The existing link table
  (`vt_surface_connections.h`, `vt_surface_boundary.h`) remains the authority
  for cross-part adjacency. Its links become edge-record destinations with the
  current slot and generation checks.
- **Removed:**
  - the seed BVH and linear seed scan (the march starts from the rasterised UV);
  - runtime reads of retained triangles.

  Keep the connected walk only as a test oracle until parity holds, then delete
  it.
- **VT from disk.** After B, POM still needs:
  - page pixels and the page edge table;
  - the chart table;
  - the receiver mapping tables (`vt_common.glsl:293-303`);
  - for terrain, the cross-part link table.

  It no longer needs triangles. Relax the lease check in
  `vt_encoded_upload.h:24-28` from triangles to charts, add the edge tables to
  the cache format (bumping its version), and then lift the forced POM-off at
  `vk_scene_renderer.cpp:6761, 13776`.

### Phase C — Skinned meshes (2 weeks; C1–C3 can run beside B)

- **C1 — VT slot for skinned draws.**
  - Write the rung's VT slot into the skin transform tail, gated on the same
    filled-tail condition as static draws (`vk_scene_renderer.cpp:7677-7695`).
  - **Demand must follow the skinned rung.** The CPU demand pass picks rungs
    from the static `cluster.radius` (`vk_scene_renderer.cpp:7440-7478`), but
    animated instances pick their rung from the animation-bounds union
    (`:16346-16353`). Mirror that selection, or the skinned rung is never
    demanded and the draw stays flat. Add a test for it.
  - Page content is rest-pose (`vt_composite.comp:316-325`), and the atlas UV
    passes through skinning unchanged (`animation_skin.comp:79-81`). Sampling
    itself needs no change.
  - Until B, disable the connected walk for skinned draws: it would march rest
    triangles against a posed ray. With A0, far fewer pixels depend on it.
- **C2 — Posed shading frame.** Two options, chosen by the parity test:
  - Skin a rest tangent packed into `VkSkinVertex`'s three padding words
    (`animation_skin.comp:33-35`). That adds no size. Transform it with the
    position matrix and re-orthogonalise.
  - Derive the rest-to-posed rotation per pixel from the posed Jacobian C3
    already computes (`pu`, `pv` in `vt_parallax_metric`) and the chart basis.

  Build the frame per pixel either way; do not interpolate one from vertices.
  `vt_normal_frame` is nonlinear and switches axis near `|n.x| > 0.999`.
  Rest-pose parity is required within tolerance, not bit for bit.
- **C3 — Metric (already posed).**
  - `vt_parallax_metric` takes posed `dFdx(in_part_local_pos)` against rest atlas
    derivatives, the posed `ray_local` and the posed normal. The normal matrix
    is instance-only (`raster.vert:395-418`).
  - Relief stays in rest metres. The error is the stretch along the normal,
    usually negligible.
  - Oracle: a skinned part rendered at rest pose must match the same part
    rendered static.
- **C4 — Posed edge frames for crossings.** Edge records carry vertex indices
  for skinned parts, so a crossing reads the posed endpoints and normals from
  the current skin output. That is two vertex reads per crossing, and it needs
  no per-texel or per-chart mapping texture. Within a chart, the per-pixel
  metric applies as today. This is the Matter-scale substitute for DASHR's
  per-frame distortion atlas.
- **C5 — Motion vectors.**
  - Start by using the shell's skinned velocity for the marched point. The error
    is bounded by the march length; the risk is TAA/DLSS ghosting.
  - The exact option is to re-evaluate the hit's surface point with the previous
    skinned positions, which the skin pass already writes. It is deferred.
- **C6 — RT.**
  - Skinned clusters are excluded from the TLAS, and their BLASes are build-once
    with no refit (`vk_scene_renderer.cpp:16383-16404`).
  - Skinned POM is therefore raster-only. Out of scope.
- **Test content.**
  - The only skinned asset is `AnimatedRigGallery`'s creature (17 joints).
  - Add a skinned analytic tube with a constant-height field and a known joint
    rotation, extending the `surface-parallax` oracle. Depth and normal
    correctness are then asserted analytically, not judged by eye.

### Phase D — Optional thick-relief shells (deferred)

Revision 1's proposal: a coarse closed cage plus a height field, replacing fine
relief triangles on displaced rocks. Consider it only after A–C, and only if VG
stabilisation leaves dense rock detail a measured cost driver. Its open risks
carry over from revision 1:
- ray-tracing seeds for procedural-AABB hits;
- marched depth output;
- 5.5–9.5 MiB of mapping per unique asset at demo formats;
- a residency contract for fields.

**On the LOD goal.** Matter has two LOD problems:
- **Geometric LOD:** triangles and silhouettes.
- **Surface-detail LOD:** relief below triangle scale.

VT height plus POM v2 is dynamic LOD for surface detail. Background compute
produces it, and after Phase B it can be served from disk. That half of the
original VT/VG goal is closest to working.

Adversarially, the gain is bounded:
- it covers relief only up to the cap (0.352 m by default);
- detail changes in mip steps;
- it does not replace VG for geometric LOD;
- it inherits VT's fill bursts and pool churn. VT Tier 1 items 1.9 (fill
  throughput) and 1.6 (pool packing) remain prerequisites for smooth results.
